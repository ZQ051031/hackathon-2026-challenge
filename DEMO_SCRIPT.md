# Dumsor Planner demo script

**Target runtime: 2 minutes 35 seconds.** Open [Dumsor Planner](https://zq051031.github.io/hackathon-2026-challenge/) in a fresh browser profile so the sample values match the narration. A fresh tab on the locally served app also works. Record at desktop width and start with the hero visible. Keep DevTools closed until the offline scene.

| Time | Click or show | Narration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:19 | Start at the hero. Show the name, then click **Plan your week**. | “In Ghana, intermittent power cuts are often called dumsor. They change when families can charge devices and when shops can finish power-dependent work. Dumsor Planner helps with both scheduling and electricity costs.” |
| 0:19–0:41 | Open **Planning area**. Choose **Kumasi**, show the changed timeline, then choose **Accra** again. Point to the sample-data notice, the Monday bars, and **Monday plan**. | “You can choose among five cities. These are sample and user-entered plans. There is no live ECG feed. Each day shows when I could charge a power bank, run higher-power equipment, or keep a small business operating around the outages I've recorded.” |
| 0:41–1:08 | Click **+ Add outage**. Set **Starts on** to **Monday**, **Start time** to **10:30**, and **End time** to **13:00**. In a 12-hour picker use 10:30 AM and 1:00 PM. Click **Save outage**. Show the **User-entered** window and the business suggestion, **13:00–18:00**. Click **Edit** beside the **10:30–13:00** window, change the end to **14:00** (2:00 PM), and save. | “If I expect an outage at ten thirty, I can add it to my plan. My longest suggested shop window now starts at one in the afternoon. When I extend the outage by an hour, the suggestion moves to two.” |
| 1:08–1:41 | Click **Energy optimizer** in the top navigation, or scroll to it. Show the **Estimate** label under the default **2.00** tariff. Click **+ Add appliance**, enter **Shop freezer**, choose **Freezer**, enter **120** watts and **6** hours/day, and click **Save appliance**. Set **Electricity tariff** to **2.50** and press Tab. Show **76.20 kWh** monthly and **GHS 190.50** monthly. | “Here are example appliances and an illustrative flat rate. I'll add a shop freezer using 120 watts for about six running hours per day, then enter two cedis fifty per kilowatt-hour as a demo input. The total is an estimated 76.2 kilowatt-hours and 190 cedis 50 pesewas in a 30-day month.” |
| 1:41–2:07 | Scroll to **Ways to save**. Point to the LED card, the fridge/freezer assumptions, and the last time-shifting card. | “The app models replacing the sample incandescent bulb with a 9-watt LED, saving about 19 cedis a month at this example rate. It also models fridge and freezer efficiency changes. Shifting work can avoid an interruption, but moving the same electricity use saves zero cedis at a flat tariff.” |
| 2:07–2:26 | Return to the top and show **Offline ready**. In Chrome open DevTools with **Ctrl+Shift+I** (macOS **Cmd+Option+I**). Open **Network** and change the throttling dropdown from **No throttling** to **Offline**. Reload normally with **Ctrl+R** (macOS **Cmd+R**). Close DevTools and show **Offline mode**, then scroll to the saved Monday window or **Shop freezer**. | “After the first load, the website works offline. I'll disconnect and reload. The outage and freezer I entered are still here because this browser saves the plan locally.” |
| 2:26–2:35 | End on the planner or optimizer. Show the GitHub repository link in the footer if time permits. | “Dumsor Planner is a static prototype for the Hackathon 2026 Challenge Electricity Efficiency track, built for households and small businesses in Ghana.” |

After recording, switch Chrome's Network throttling dropdown back to **No throttling**.
